# Khaervazynth

> The key was carved from a promise. Every door remembered a different voice.

## The Locksmith's Unfinished House

Khaervazynth stands upon a narrow island where the river runs uphill after sunset. Its house has nineteen doors and no walls between them. From a distance, travelers see only wooden frames surrounding a table of dark stone.

Upon the table rests a key. Its teeth resemble small horns, and its handle contains a hollow through which an unfamiliar room can be seen. The room is furnished differently for every observer, but always contains a person sleeping with one hand closed.

The locksmith lives somewhere among the doors. Nobody agrees which doorway leads to his chamber. Those who knock hear the same answer from every frame: the promise is still being measured.

## A Guest with a Copper Tongue

A guest arrived carrying a small copper leaf beneath his tongue. He could speak only when the leaf was placed upon the table. His words sounded like wind moving through a hall of glass.

He asked the locksmith to open a door that had appeared inside his dreams. Its handle was warm. Its hinges were hidden. From behind it came the sound of wings folding around a sleeping child.

The locksmith offered him the key. The guest looked through its hollow and recognized the room from his dream, except that the child now held the copper leaf in a closed hand.

He returned the key without trying a door. The leaf disappeared from the table. When he spoke again, every doorway answered in his voice, although he had asked nothing.

The locksmith left one frame covered until morning. Beneath the cloth, a long shadow moved across the island without touching the river.

## The Ledger of Unopened Rooms

The house contains a ledger whose entries describe rooms that have never been entered. Each account begins with the weight of a promise and ends with the name of a witness who has not arrived.

Some promises weigh less than breath. Others require two people to lift the ledger from the table. One entry has no weight at all, but causes the island to sink slightly whenever its page is turned.

A scholar attempted to find the matching room. She carried the ledger through each doorway, discovering streets, fields, and a corridor paved with copper leaves. At its end stood a person holding an empty cloth.

The person asked which frame she had uncovered. The scholar answered with the number nineteen. The corridor darkened, and she found herself beside the table with a copper mark upon her palm.

The ledger's weightless entry had acquired her handwriting.

## The Shape beneath the Island

During the river's upward passage, something pale can be seen beneath the island. Its body is long and scaled, curled around a hollow large enough to contain the locksmith's house.

Its wings lie folded against the riverbed. Between their bones are narrow spaces resembling doorframes. Through those spaces, observers glimpse the nineteen doors standing upon the table instead of around it.

A fisherman lowered a hook toward the creature. The hook returned carrying a wooden hinge, still warm, with a small horn growing from its upper edge.

He brought it to the locksmith. The man placed the hinge beside the key and asked the fisherman which promise he had intended to catch.

The fisherman could remember only a closed hand. The locksmith covered a second doorway. That night, two shadows moved beneath the cloths, each folding wings the river could not reflect.

## The Words inside the Key

**Khaer — va — zynth — orel.**

These sounds are heard when the key's hollow is held toward an unopened door. They emerge in different voices, but always leave the same brief silence after the final syllable.

The locksmith places that silence in the ledger by closing its cover before the echo ends. No ink is needed. The next page acquires a drawing of a hand holding something too small to identify.

A visitor tried to prolong the final sound. The river stopped moving. Beneath the island, the creature opened one eye, revealing the room inside the key from an impossible distance.

The visitor released his breath. The river resumed its ascent, and a third frame acquired a cloth. The locksmith's ledger began closing by itself whenever that visitor approached.

## A Promise Spoken across the Table

> Which door belongs to the sleeping hand?
>
> The one whose handle has not learned its weight.
>
> Who measures the promise beneath the cloth?
>
> The witness who left a voice upon the island.
>
> Why does the key show a room instead of a passage?
>
> Because someone has already entered from the other side.

These replies were preserved by the scholar on a copper leaf found inside the ledger. The leaf grew warm whenever its final line was read aloud.

She placed it beneath her tongue. For an evening, the doorways answered in her voice. She waited until they fell silent before attempting to remove it.

## The Nineteenth Door

The house remains unfinished. Its frames contain no walls, and the locksmith continues measuring promises that arrive without witnesses.

The covered doors stand facing the river. Sometimes the cloths lift without wind, revealing a copper corridor or a pale eye. Nobody has recorded what appears behind the nineteenth frame.

Khaervazynth waits upon the island that descends while its river climbs. Inside the key, a sleeping person loosens one finger, and the promise upon the table becomes lighter than the voice that first carried it.
